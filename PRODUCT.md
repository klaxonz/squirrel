# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Squirrel is for a desktop user who keeps video subscriptions, RSS sources, music playback, history, site runtimes, scheduled jobs, logs, profile, and settings in one private workspace. They use it during long browsing and listening sessions, so the UI must be quiet, predictable, and fast to scan rather than expressive for its own sake.

## Product Purpose

Squirrel exists as a personal media and content control surface. Success means the user can move between recommendation, library, subscription, music, reader, playback, and system-management tasks without losing context or encountering surprising navigation behavior.

## Brand Personality

Quiet, cinematic, competent. The interface should feel like a private media archive with operational controls: dark-first viewing comfort, restrained amber identity, soft atmospheric depth, and compact desktop affordances. It should make content feel organized and recoverable without turning task screens into marketing pages.

## Anti-references

Avoid browser-like back/forward chrome as a primary mental model, streaming-service billboard layouts, generic SaaS dashboards, oversized CTAs, animated spectacle, and inconsistent one-off component styling across modules. Atmospheric depth, soft glow, and light glass treatment are allowed when they improve media focus and match the login entry surface.

## Design Principles

- Product hierarchy beats browser history: visible navigation should describe app structure and current context first.
- Content is the lead object: imagery can carry video and music surfaces, but controls, labels, and filters stay product-grade and consistent.
- One control vocabulary: buttons, tabs, menus, inputs, empty states, overlays, and focus states should look related across video, music, RSS, settings, and playback.
- Desktop density is intentional: optimize for a large-screen app shell with stable spacing, not mobile-first stacking.
- State must be visible: loading, empty, error, disabled, selected, hover, active, and focus states should be clear without modal or toast overuse.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG AA contrast for text and controls. Keyboard focus must be visible on every interactive surface. Motion should be short, state-driven, and respect reduced-motion preferences. Use text plus shape/state, not color alone, for important status.
